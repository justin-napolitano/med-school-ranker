# Critical Review: Site Visual Direction

## Finding 1: Youth-Oriented Design Can Become Gimmicky

Targeting Gen Z and Gen Alpha does not mean using slang, neon palettes, or novelty interactions.

Mitigation:

- Use modern interaction density and transparency rather than forced youth branding.
- Keep copy direct and product-like.

## Finding 2: Serious Design Can Drift Into Institutional Design

Medical and education products often default to navy, gray, generic tables, and brochure-like pages.

Mitigation:

- Use cards, confidence chips, compact personalization controls, and modern hierarchy.
- Keep admin/status pages visually separate from applicant-facing pages.

## Finding 3: Design Could Hide Uncertainty

Polished cards can make weak or missing data look authoritative.

Mitigation:

- Treat missing, partial, provisional, and low-confidence states as first-class design elements.
- Put source confidence and caveats near the affected values.

## Finding 4: Mobile Could Become A Secondary Adaptation

Applicants will review and shortlist schools across devices. A desktop-first layout squeezed onto mobile will feel wrong.

Mitigation:

- Design card anatomy mobile-first.
- Keep rank, fit, cost, confidence, and actions visible in the first mobile scan.

## Finding 5: Style Implementation Is Premature Before Node Adoption

The design direction is ready, but full implementation should wait until card/profile nodes are consumed by the UI.

Mitigation:

- Document style now.
- Implement visual system after the profile-node UI adoption slice proves the card/profile rendering contract.

## Go/No-Go Assessment

This visual direction is ready as a planning artifact. Full style implementation should not start until the profile-node UI adoption slice is complete.
